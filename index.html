Each column is one view axis written in the true coordinates. A pure Mandelbrot view has x = Re c and y = Im c.

z2 + c in ℂ²

The set that Mandelbrot and Julia sets are slices of.

Iterating f(z) = z² + c involves two complex numbers: the parameter c and the starting point z₀. Collect every pair (c, z₀) whose orbit z₀, f(z₀), f(f(z₀)), … stays bounded, and you get a single object in ℂ², a four‑dimensional real space.

Re cIm cRe z₀Im z₀
  • The Mandelbrot set is its slice through z₀ = 0.
  • Each filled Julia set is its slice at one fixed c.
  • Every other plane, and every 3D hyperplane, cuts out a relative of the two. This explorer lets you place and tilt those cuts.

How the view is set up

The view carries four perpendicular axes x, y, z, w and a point P. The 2D view shows the plane through P spanned by x and y; the inset shows the perpendicular plane through P, spanned by z and w. In the Mandelbrot view that inset is exactly the Julia set of the c under the marker. The 3D view shows the hyperplane through P spanned by x, y, z; w is the hidden direction. The compass in the corner shows how the four true axes project onto your screen.

2D view

  • Drag to pan; pinch, scroll or press +/− to zoom; tap to move P; double‑tap to zoom in 3×. “Zoom in toward P” under Animate dives continuously.
  • Drag the dot in the inset to move P across the perpendicular plane, for example to change z₀ while looking at the Mandelbrot view.
  • Tilt in 4D (or hold Shift while dragging): sideways turns x toward z, up and down turns y toward w. Add Alt to pair x with w and y with z instead.

3D view

  • Drag to orbit; pinch or scroll to move closer; two fingers or right‑drag to pan; double‑tap the surface to orbit around that spot.
  • Tilt in 4D (or Shift‑drag) turns the hyperplane through the hidden w axis, so the solid itself changes shape.
  • “Cut open” removes the half facing you and reveals the 2D slice through P as a cross‑section.

Rendering is progressive: a quick pass while you move, then full resolution with anti‑aliasing once you stop. Past about 10⁵× the 2D view switches to high precision: one reference orbit is computed exactly in JavaScript and the GPU only tracks each pixel’s small difference from it (perturbation with rebasing). That reaches about 10³⁰×. You can type P with as many digits as you like. The 3D view stays in single precision. The view is stored in the page address, so “Copy link” saves it.
